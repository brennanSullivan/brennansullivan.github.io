I am a postdoc with Nuo Li at Duke Neurobiology, where I focus on motor learning. Previously, I completed my Ph.D. with Jeff Magee at Baylor College of Medicine, where I used in vivo whole-cell recording in behaving mice to investigate rapid, dendritic plateau-dependent plasticity in neocortical pyramidal neurons.

My long-term goal is to understand how the brain can modify itself through experience to produce adaptive behavior. I approach this problem by investigating how neocortical neurons process their inputs, modify their own input-output relationship, and generate outputs that drive learning. There are three primary motivations for pursuing this problem: to advance knowledge, to accelerate rational treatments for neuropsychiatric disease, and to inspire new designs for intelligent systems.
